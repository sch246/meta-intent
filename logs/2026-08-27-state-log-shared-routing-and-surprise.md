# STATE/LOG shared routing and structural surprise

## Clarified candidate

Earlier records established two independent directions: source LOGs may need derived indexes when linear history becomes too large, and STATE may organize higher- and lower-order intent. The new candidate unifies their addressing without merging their authority:

Source provenance and current normative meaning remain different kinds of information.

The candidate does not imply one persistent graph. It predicts a shared route that could later be realized by links, derived indexes, lazy views or another structure if evidence requires one.

## Query and assimilation

Before an operation, the available values are the current STATE hierarchy, selected source records, any replaceable navigation views, the current question and checked reality scope. During the operation, an Agent derives a relevant path, an interpretation of what the existing path already explains, the residual introduced by the new information and the smallest semantically sufficient repair it can identify.

The candidate separates three writes:

- source recording preserves an event when it has causal, evidence or authority value;
- derived assimilation updates replaceable navigation and abstractions according to what the current route could not already absorb;
- normative STATE changes only through appropriate authority when desired meaning, acceptance, permissions or another STATE-owned concern changes.

Surprise is therefore not a stored score. It is a situated property of an assimilation: the new information forces a change in what the current route distinguishes, predicts or can answer. The repair is relative to the absorbing Agent and its working set, so neither repair size nor a claim that something was surprising becomes portable authority.

## Repetition and bounded hot representation

Repeated information should not repeatedly cause the same full investigation. A duplicate that again reaches durable recording often indicates that the existing record or route was not discovered soon enough. Repetition should make the relevant route easier to hit and permit an earlier no-change or bounded-difference result. In this operational sense, a shorter fast-fail route is one form of a deep impression.

Repetition still has provenance value. The user proposed a candidate progression: the first occurrence receives a complete account; a later occurrence records its difference and time; subsequent occurrences retain only novel content plus increasingly compact recurrence evidence; stable repetition may eventually update only a count and bounded recent pattern. Accumulated evidence may reveal that a logical record should merge with another record or split into distinct cases.

This proposal creates a direct protocol tension. Protocol 0.2 makes selected LOG files immutable once committed or bound, and accepted LOCKs bind their bytes by digest. A successor could instead distinguish a stable logical-record identity and mutable current aggregate from immutable historical revisions, but no storage model, compaction policy or migration has been selected. Bounding the hot representation while retaining historical revisions is different from bounding total storage. Total-storage bounds necessarily lose some future ability to reinterpret occurrences that were previously judged equivalent.

Recurrence statistics are observations, not user preference or normative intent. They may be attached to navigation beneath a relevant STATE node, but frequency and surprise cannot promote one STATE branch, manufacture importance or enter normative STATE merely because they influence retrieval cost.

## Preserved tensions

### Relevance before learnability

Structural change reflects novelty and current parseability, not user importance. A clear but unresolved acceptance failure may require little conceptual repair while an irrelevant refactor may produce a rich sequence of distinctions. STATE relevance therefore bounds the eligible work; surprise may affect attention only inside the user-relevant scope. Otherwise frequency and novelty can be laundered into an inferred preference despite the current prohibition.

### Local succession instead of unrecorded old queries

The phrase "old queries remain reachable" has no direct object unless queries themselves are retained. The local candidate property is narrower: an assimilation preserves source attribution and explicit succession rather than overwriting a conclusion, so an old conclusion can still reach what superseded it. Whether this is sufficient for arbitrary historical query recovery remains unproved.

### Intent hierarchy and cycles

The complete relation network may have multiple parents and cycles. The higher-order `serves` or `derives-from` relation cannot contain an unresolved cycle while retaining its current meaning: `META-013` requires recovery of the higher-order intent served. Shared lower-order intent may therefore form a DAG; support, contradiction, similarity, succession and retrieval relations are not constrained by that hierarchy and may cycle.

### Situated repair

The repair produced by assimilation is not a deterministic function of the stored network and input. Different Agents or working sets may form different abstractions and repair different paths. Repair can guide current attention and produce inspectable results, but it cannot establish that the information was universally important or surprising.

### Stable failure is required for hot-input suppression

Repeated but currently incomprehensible material naturally fast-fails only if the system can form a reusable description of the obstruction. Unrelated noise does not guarantee its own suppression. If no stable failure pattern is recoverable, an external context or cost bound remains necessary; the candidate must not claim that a Goldilocks region always emerges.

## Falsifiable sequence

A qualifying experiment begins from current STATE and selected LOGs without a prescribed persistent graph. It presents four classes of input in sequence and requires a later maintenance judgment to consume the resulting working set:

1. an exact or semantic duplicate should reach an existing route and require decreasing derived repair rather than another full investigation;
2. partially understood, incrementally novel inputs should produce bounded local refinements and keep exposing a next resolvable distinction;
3. repeated but currently unresolved inputs should form a reusable obstruction and then converge to an early bounded result;
4. a later environment change that can affect that obstruction should reopen it, while unrelated drift should not.

The experiment must also include two adversarial controls. A novel but STATE-irrelevant input must not displace a dull current acceptance failure. After several occurrences have been treated as equivalent, a distinction that depends on earlier time or environment must test whether the retained evidence can split the aggregate correctly; failure demonstrates that the proposed compression discarded a meaning-bearing difference.

Observe selected routes, source coverage, derived edits, succession links, downstream judgment, false fast-fails, context cost and differences from a flat append-only baseline. The sequence falsifies the explanation if repetitions keep causing full work, unresolved difficulty never forms a reusable boundary, relevant change cannot reopen it, or novelty displaces STATE-owned importance.
