## Surprise and attention correction

The earlier record described second-order surprise as prediction error decreasing after a STATE correction and proposed an explicit error series. That formulation introduced an evaluator-shaped metric that was not the user's intended mechanism.

The user's clarification is endogenous:

- surprise supplies attention when reality defeats current expectation;
- learning can sustain attention by creating further distinctions and expectations that make further surprise possible;
- second-order surprise concerns that continuation of surprise through learning, not a second judge deciding whether the first learning was good;
- loss of attention does not prove a claim false, a concern unimportant, or a difficulty permanently closed;
- the effort already spent and the failure to produce further surprise are themselves situated history. Encountering similar history again makes the same difficulty less novel, and repeated histories may naturally be generalized during retrieval;
- such a generalization remains sensitive to the environment in which the difficulty occurred. A later change reopens investigation only when it can bear on the earlier obstruction; unrelated change does not reset the history;
- surprise cannot guarantee that learning is not noise. Genuinely difficult learning may fail to sustain attention, and the user accepts that limitation as corresponding to real learning rather than authorizing an external evaluator to manufacture persistence.

These effects should arise from preserved source history and from using the current question and reality to read and extend that history. This record does not require a difficulty schema, surprise score, retry counter, fixed graph, mechanical reopen predicate, or new validator. A derived summary or cached view remains replaceable evidence about reading, never current intent authority.

Zero observed surprise remains ambiguous when changing reality was excluded from observation. Observation scope may therefore be investigated before interpreting absence, but coverage evidence judges what was observed; it does not judge whether learning deserves authority.
